Commentary

  • Europe/Mediterranean
    A rather unexciting week for LR1s. MRs have continued to soften to the lowest level in two years (37kt ARA/USAC concluded at WS90), making it difficult for LR1s to compete on USD/PMT on intra-west runs and muting demand for LR1s to West Africa or South America. There are, however, still charterers seeking to move gasoline or clean condensate to the Middle East on LR1s, but the amount of demand is not going to move the market, with only two firm cargoes being quoted, this week. Tonnage remains in short supply, but owners will be mindful of finding a way out of the West, so we expect the market to stay flat, at best.
  • Far East
    MR market finally started improving, which will invite Charterers to explore for bigger size. Despite no natural LR1 cargo in North Asia, sentiments are improving on the back of an improved AG market. Korea to Singapore freight assessment improved for MRs to $650k. While LR1 & LR2 remains unchanged at $700k for this week with a lack of activity but theoretical assessment should improve to the range of $750-800k considering MRs and the front haul market. On long haul business, LR2s are preferred for Australia, but MRs for westbound. In South East Asia, a couple of gasoline cargoes worked loading from Singapore region to discharge in Pakistan and Indonesia.
  • Americas
    It has been a week without a single LR1 fixture, but it was not for a lack of interest or inquiries quoted, but merely a lack of charterers able to commit to firm barrels. A are LR2 cargo on the other hand was confirmed, a sharp WS95 for TA. On the DPP markets, the rates have remained more or less flat at the bottom throughout the week; Aframaxes still around WS100 for TA and very low 80s for short hauls in the Americas, and Panamaxes around the WS130 mark for ECMex to USG. Limited downside if any on especially the larger size where TCEs are nearing zero, whereas Panamaxes might still have some room to drop.
  • Middle East
    As outlined in last week’s report, we anticipated freight increases across all routes based on the positive fundamentals and sentiment from last week. We have seen TC5 (LR1 AG/Japan) increase from WS157.5 to WS170 and TC1 (LR2 AG/Japan) increase from WS140 to WS150. The LR1 and LR2 remain positive in isolation, but the soft MR segment is currently creating a glass ceiling for both segments. At the end of this week, we have seen a handful of cargoes being split into MR size and expect this to be the ongoing trend next week as the MR segment is cheaper USD/MT. Both LR1 and LR2 are currently capped at current levels because of the soft MR segment and we need strong overperformance to turn this around. Until then, we don’t expect any upside for the LR1 and LR2 segments but call the market flat at current levels with a slightly negative sentiment caused by the MR segment.
